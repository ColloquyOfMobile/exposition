# A GF1002 amplifier module was destroyed instantly by +12 V.
# The wiring was correct. The document was wrong.

WHAT HAPPENED

test audio at 12v -> measure at 12 V, on the bench (DESKTOP-MRSLS88),
with Thomas's audio subsystem on the desk. The +12 V was brought to one
GF1002 amplifier module's VDD/GND screw terminal, as SUPPLY_SETUP.md
section 3 instructs. The module died the instant the rail came up.

Nothing else was damaged. The Mega, the filter board, the MSGEQ7
analyser array and the MAX9814 microphones all still answer.


WHAT WAS WRONG

Not the wiring, and not the person. A number with no source.

  SUPPLY_SETUP.md:57  "The amplifier module is specified 4.5-15 V, so
                       any of the three is safe for *it*"
  NEXT_PCB.md:209     "amplifier module | class-D, rated **4.5-15 V**"

Neither had a citation. `git log -S` puts the claim in 93b4e98 ("Decide
the amplifier rail"), copied into the supply document by 3629eb3
("Measure what 12 V buys"). There is no datasheet for the GF1002 in this
repository, no part marking recorded, and no measurement behind the
range. Every other hard number here is traceable to a source - the baud
rate to a #define in the sketch, the mirror range to TJ's OpenCM
firmware, every pinout to the KiCad copper. This one was asserted.


THE DEFECT WORTH REMEMBERING

The two sentences above are not the same claim, and the slide between
them is what put 12 V on the board.

In NEXT_PCB.md section 5 the range is a **specification for a module
still to be bought** - the section even ends "confirm it against the
module actually chosen". SUPPLY_SETUP.md then reused it as a **statement
of fact about the modules already on the bench**. A specification for a
part you intend to buy and a description of the part in your hand read
identically and are opposite in kind: one is a requirement you impose,
the other is a property you have to go and check.

The GF1002's actual rating is still not known here. What the bench has
now established is that it is a 5 V-class part.


WHAT WAS CHANGED

- params > audio gained "amplifier module" and "amplifier max supply
  volts" (GF1002, 5.0). 5.0 is NOT a datasheet maximum - it is the rail
  the module has always run on and survived, and the comment in
  params.py says so.
- test audio at 12v now refuses any pass above the recorded rating,
  instantly, reading only that number. `measure at 5 V` still runs;
  `measure at 12 V` refuses and says why. Raising the number is a
  deliberate press against a datasheet, never against another document
  in this repo.
- Both false claims are struck from SUPPLY_SETUP.md and NEXT_PCB.md.
- NEXT_PCB.md section 5's rail decision is reopened. Its reasoning about
  current is untouched by this, but it was written assuming the existing
  amplifiers could be carried over to the rail, and they cannot.


WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWN

- The GF1002's real supply range. Read it off the module or its
  listing before anything else is decided about it.
- Whether the failure was short or open, and in which direction. If it
  failed short from VDD to its input, the Mega timer pin behind the
  22K/3K3 divider saw 12 V through 22K. The Mega answers, so this is
  very likely fine - but the pin that was driving that channel is worth
  metering before it is trusted for a measurement.
